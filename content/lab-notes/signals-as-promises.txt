title: Signals as Promises (and why timing is moral)
date: 2026-05-27

excerpt: A mental model: a signal is a promise you make to another part of the circuit.
---

[text]
A digital signal looks clean on paper: 0 or 1, high or low. In reality it’s more like a promise made under constraints: rise time, noise, impedance, and the fact that the world is analog.

[text]
When a microcontroller pin flips, it’s not teleporting a value. It’s pushing charge into a network of capacitances and resistances. The “1” arrives with a shape, not a label.

[heading] The promise

[list]
- Voltage level: “I will be above this threshold (most of the time).”
- Timing: “I will be stable when you sample.”
- Drive strength: “I can source/sink enough current without collapsing.”
- Noise margin: “Small chaos won’t change the meaning.”

[heading] A small equation I actually use

[text]
RC rise time isn’t a textbook detail — it’s the reason your edges become mush:

[latex]
\tau = R \cdot C

[text]
If your line has effective capacitance \(C\), and your output has effective resistance \(R\), then the edge is a slow negotiation, not a snap.

[heading] Practical habit

[list]
- When something “randomly” fails, suspect timing before you suspect logic.
- Probe the line, not the idea.
- Add a pull-up or a small RC only after you understand what it’s fixing.

[quote]
A clean schematic is a prayer. A scope trace is a confession.
